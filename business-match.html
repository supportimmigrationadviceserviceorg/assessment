Questions people ask first
What is this visa, exactly?

The United States gives a visa to people who buy or start a business there and run it themselves. It is called the E-2. You do not need a job offer, a degree, or a relative in the country. You need a real business that you own and are actually in charge of.

You can bring your partner and your children under 21. Your partner can work at any job. It can be renewed again and again for as long as the business is running.

And not everyone can do this. The route only exists between the United States and countries it has a treaty with. Fewer than half the countries in the world have one. Most of Europe, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Canada, Australia and Israel are in. Several of the largest countries in the world are not, and their citizens have no version of this at all.

If your country is on the list, you have a door open to you that most people on earth do not have. Tell us where you hold citizenship and we will confirm it in a sentence.

Do I have to know how to run this kind of business?

No. Most buyers have never worked in the trade they end up buying. Sellers normally stay on for a few weeks to show you how everything works, and businesses that already have trained staff make it easier still.

How much money do I really need?

The price of the business, plus roughly $15,000 to $30,000 for legal fees, the visa itself and running costs in the first months. There is no official minimum. The cheaper the business, the more of its price you are expected to put in yourself.

Do I have to live where the business is?

Near it, yes. You have to be genuinely running it rather than owning it from abroad. That is why most people look at what is for sale first and choose a city afterwards.

Can my family come with me?

Your husband or wife and any children under 21 come with you. Your partner can apply to work anywhere, not only in your business. Children go to school as normal.
